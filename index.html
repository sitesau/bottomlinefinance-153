Private Lending Explained

Mainstream banks assess applications using a rigid, standardised scorecard and a fixed credit policy. In the non-bank sector, the assessment is fundamentally different. Lenders evaluate the specific merits of the transaction, focusing intensely on the underlying asset, the security position, the borrower's track record, and a credible exit strategy. This distinction is critical for property developers and asset-backed investors whose projects are fundamentally sound but fall outside traditional bank appetite. Reasons for rejection often include credit policy constraints, timing issues, or leverage limits. By examining the deal itself rather than applying a generic checklist, private creates a pathway to capital that mainstream institutions cannot provide. It acknowledges that a strong deal with a complex story deserves funding even if it does not tick every box on a standard form. This deal-by-deal philosophy is the core advantage for borrowers who need a lender to look at the opportunity rather than just the algorithm.

Security Structures and Risk Pricing

Security is the primary consideration in these transactions. Lenders require tangible assets to mitigate risk, typically taking a registered first or second mortgage over property. In some urgent, short-term scenarios, a caveat loan may be used to secure funds against the title almost immediately. Pricing is not standardised like a bank variable rate; it is customised to the risk profile of the specific deal. The main drivers of cost include the loan-to-value ratio (LVR), the quality and location of the asset, and the certainty of the exit strategy. A low-geared deal with a solid exit and a prime asset will attract a more competitive rate than a highly leveraged position with a secondary security. This risk-adjusted pricing compensates the lender for the increased flexibility and speed of settlement. Borrowers must understand that the rate reflects the bespoke nature of the facility and the complexity of the credit assessment, which often requires manual underwriting rather than automated approval.

Commercial Applications and Loan Variants

This form of finance is not a single product but a suite of commercial tools designed for specific situations. Bridging finance is used to close a timing gap, allowing a borrower to settle a purchase before another asset sells. Construction loans fund the build in progress, with capital released against specific milestones rather than as a lump sum. Mezzanine finance sits behind senior debt in the capital stack, providing additional leverage for developers seeking to maximise returns. Caveat loans offer very short-term, urgent funding, while residual stock loans refinance completed dwellings that remain unsold after practical completion. Second mortgages can be used to unlock additional equity from an existing property. Each of these structures serves a distinct purpose in the development and investment cycle. They are tailored to the cash flow and asset position of the borrower, ensuring that the funding aligns with the business strategy rather than forcing the business strategy to fit the loan terms.

Strategic Use and Borrower Profile

The appropriate borrower profile includes experienced property developers, commercial investors, and builders. These professionals often require funding that exceeds the conservative LVR limits of banks or needs to settle within tight deadlines. It is also highly relevant for rural and agribusiness operators, where the specialised nature of the security does not align with standard residential or commercial bank templates. The strategic value of this capital lies in its ability to preserve equity and improve return on equity. By moving quickly or structuring a deal that banks cannot service, borrowers can secure opportunities that would otherwise be lost. It is crucial to note that this is commercial funding only. It is not appropriate for personal borrowing or owner-occupied residential homes. For those specific needs, traditional banking channels are the correct path. This sector exists to support commercial enterprise and asset-backed growth, bridging the gap where policy constraints stop progress.

  1. Assess the Asset. Evaluate the quality and value of the security property, ensuring it is residential, commercial, industrial or specialised and has a clear market value.
  2. Define the Exit. Establish a credible exit strategy, such as the sale of the asset, refinance to a standard bank, or completion of a development project.
  3. Structure the Loan. Determine the appropriate structure, such as a first mortgage, caveat loan or mezzanine finance, based on the risk profile and required leverage.
  4. Approve and Settle. Submit the deal for assessment; once approved by the lender, proceed to settlement, which can often occur within days to weeks.

Common questions

Why are interest rates higher than bank rates? Rates are higher because the risk is higher and the underwriting is more intensive. Banks enjoy cheap funding from depositors. Private lenders use capital that costs more, and they take on deals that banks deem too complex or risky. The pricing also reflects the speed of settlement and the flexibility of terms. You are paying for a bespoke solution that solves a specific problem, such as a time constraint or a complex asset class, rather than a mass-market product.

What security is required for approval? Lenders typically require a registered mortgage over real property, either as a first or second ranking position. The property must have sufficient value to cover the loan amount, usually assessed at a conservative loan-to-value ratio. In some cases, other assets or a caveat may be used, but the core requirement is a tangible, realizable asset that can be sold if the exit strategy fails. The security position is the primary factor in determining if the deal can proceed.

How fast can settlement be achieved? One of the primary advantages is speed. While a bank might take 45 to 60 days, a private lender can often settle within days to a few weeks, provided the valuation and legal documentation are in order. This speed is possible because the decision is made by experienced credit assessors looking at the specific deal, rather than a committee adhering to a rigid policy framework. This makes it ideal for auction purchases or urgent refinancing needs.
